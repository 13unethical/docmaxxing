"How do I bypass Turnitin?" is what students type after a draft comes back with a high AI score, or before they submit something they wrote with ChatGPT, Grammarly or QuillBot. Turnitin's AI writing report estimates how much of your prose looks machine-written, and a high number is what teachers see.

There is no switch that makes any text invisible to Turnitin. The score moves when the sentences that look AI-written stop looking that way. This is the sequence that actually changes it.

## What Turnitin's AI score is looking at

The percentage is the share of your **qualifying prose** that the model predicts was written by a language model. It is not a probability that "you cheated," and it does not cover the whole file: headings, bibliographies, tables and very short lines are usually left out.

Two things push a score up:

- **Predictable phrasing.** Even rhythm, stock transitions ("Furthermore," "It is important to note"), and sentences that all land at the same length.
- **AI that was only lightly edited.** Swapping a few synonyms, or running a basic paraphraser, often leaves the sentence structure intact. Newer Turnitin reports can mark text as "AI-generated, then paraphrased," so a synonym pass is not a bypass.

A low score is shown as **\*%** rather than a number. That asterisk means Turnitin found only a small amount of AI-like text — we explain it in [What does \*% mean in Turnitin's AI report?](/blog/turnitin-ai-score-asterisk).

## The sequence that lowers the score

**1. See which sentences are the problem.**
A single percentage doesn't tell you what to fix. Paste the draft into the [free AI detector](/ai-detector) and read the highlights. Those sentences are the ones to rewrite. Our detector is trained against real Turnitin reports, so it is a useful preview. It is not the official report — if you need that, run the Turnitin check itself.

**2. Rewrite the flagged sentences, not the whole voice of the essay.**
The sentences that score as AI are the ones with the template rhythm. Rewrite them the way you'd explain the point to a classmate: different sentence lengths, a concrete detail from the reading or the lecture, your own verb instead of "utilize" and "leverage." Leave headings, numbers and citations alone.

Doing this by hand is the most reliable version. If the flagged stretch is long, the [AI humanizer](/humanizer) rewrites the passage into natural prose while keeping the argument, the numbers and the in-text citations. It is free for short texts. Read every sentence it returns — an automatic rewrite can drop a nuance, and a sentence you don't understand is one you can't defend.

**3. Check again before you submit.**
Run the rewritten text through the detector. If a paragraph is still highlighted, rewrite that paragraph only and check once more. Stop when the highlights are gone or down to a line or two, not when a tool tells you the score is "zero."

## What does not work

- **Synonym spinners and one-click paraphrasers.** They change words and keep the shape of the sentence, which is exactly what "AI-paraphrased" detection is built for. The text also starts to read strangely, which is its own problem in a seminar.
- **Adding invisible characters, white text or weird spacing.** Turnitin extracts the text. Tricks in the file formatting don't change the sentences, and a teacher who pastes your essay into a blank document watches them disappear.
- **Quoting a promise of 0%.** Detectors are updated, they disagree with each other, and they sometimes flag writing a person did from scratch. Anyone guaranteeing a number is guessing.

## Before you rely on a score

Check your course rules. Plenty of classes allow AI for brainstorming or editing and want that said in the paper; some allow none of it. A low AI score is not permission, and a high one is not proof. If a teacher asks, your outlines, notes and the version history in Google Docs or Word are what actually shows the work is yours.

## Key takeaways

- Turnitin scores qualifying prose, and it can mark text that was only paraphrased.
- Lower the score by rewriting the highlighted sentences in your own rhythm, then checking again.
- The [free AI detector](/ai-detector) shows which lines to fix; the [humanizer](/humanizer) rewrites a long flagged passage for you.
- Read the result, keep your sources, and don't trust a tool that promises a specific percentage.
