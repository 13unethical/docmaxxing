A good Turnitin AI score is the one your instructor is willing to accept. Turnitin does not publish a pass mark. In practice, **\*%** (the asterisk that replaces a low number) rarely starts a conversation, a score around **20%** sometimes does, and **50% or higher** is what gets a draft sent back.

The percentage is an estimate of how much of your qualifying prose looks machine-written. It is not proof that you used AI, and it is not a grade.

## How to read the number

- **No AI score.** The submission was too short, or the institution has the indicator turned off.
- **\*%.** Turnitin found only a small amount of AI-like text, roughly the band under 20%, and chose not to print an exact figure. Details: [what \*% means](/blog/turnitin-ai-score-asterisk).
- **About 20–40%.** A real share of the prose looks AI-like or AI-then-paraphrased. Some teachers ask about it, many don't.
- **50% and up.** Most of the qualifying sentences were scored as AI-like. Expect a question, and sometimes a resubmit.
- **80–100%.** The draft reads like an unedited chatbot answer from start to finish.

Only long-form sentences count. Headings, the reference list, tables and very short lines are usually skipped, so a 15-page document full of bullet points can show a lower score than a tight 1,000-word essay.

## What teachers actually do with it

Turnitin tells instructors not to treat the score as the only evidence. Many still use a personal cutoff: anything with an asterisk is fine, anything over a third gets a look, anything over a half gets a meeting. You will not see that cutoff in the student view. If you can see a number at all, a teacher or a separate check shared it with you.

A low score does not make AI use allowed. A high score does not prove it. Course rules decide the first question; the report only starts the second.

## How to see the score before they do

Students usually cannot open their own Turnitin AI report. Preview the draft anyway:

1. Paste it into the [free AI detector](/ai-detector). It highlights the sentences that look AI-written, which is the part a high percentage is made of. The model is tuned on real Turnitin reports, so treat the highlights as a preview, not as Turnitin's official number.
2. Rewrite those sentences. Vary the length, put in a detail from the reading or the lecture, and cut the stock transitions ("Furthermore," "It is important to note"). A long flagged passage can go through the [AI humanizer](/humanizer); read the result and fix anything that changed your point.
3. Run the detector again. Stop when the highlights are down to a line or two.

The same steps are how people [bring a Turnitin AI score down](/blog/how-to-bypass-turnitin-ai-detection) before the deadline, instead of arguing about it after.

## If the number is already high

Don't resubmit the same file with a few synonyms swapped. Turnitin can mark text as AI-generated and then paraphrased, so a surface rewrite often stays in the same band. Rewrite the highlighted paragraphs, keep your sources, and be ready to show the version history. If you wrote it yourself and it was still flagged, that happens — [what to do when human writing is flagged](/blog/essay-flagged-as-ai-but-i-wrote-it).

## Key takeaways

- **\*%** is the result teachers rarely chase. **50%+** is the result they do.
- There is no official pass mark. The number covers qualifying prose only.
- Check the draft with the [free AI detector](/ai-detector), rewrite what it highlights, and check again before you submit.
