If you've seen a Turnitin AI writing report with **\*%** instead of a number, you're not alone. It's one of the most searched questions about Turnitin, and the short answer is reassuring: the asterisk means the detector found only a small amount of text it considers AI-like — too little for Turnitin to show a reliable number.

Below is what the asterisk means, why Turnitin added it, and what you can do if a teacher asks about your report.

## The short answer

Turnitin's AI indicator estimates **what share of your qualifying text** looks AI-generated. When that estimate falls in a low range (Turnitin has described it as roughly 1–19%), the report shows \*% instead of the exact figure and doesn't highlight sentences.

Turnitin did this on purpose. At low percentages, false positives — human sentences wrongly flagged — make up a large part of what the detector finds. Printing "12%" would suggest a precision the tool doesn't have, so it shows an asterisk instead.

So \*% is **not** a hidden high score, and it is **not** proof that you used AI.

## What the AI percentage actually measures

The number is easy to misread, so it's worth being precise:

- It is **not** the probability that you used AI.
- It is **not** the share of your *whole* document. Only "qualifying" text counts — long-form prose sentences. Headings, lists, tables, references and very short lines are usually skipped.
- It is the share of those qualifying sentences that the model predicts were written by a language model (newer reports may split this into "AI-generated" and "AI-generated, then paraphrased").

That's also why very short submissions often get no AI score at all: Turnitin needs a minimum amount of prose before it will run the check.

## Who can see the AI report?

In most setups, the AI writing report is visible to instructors and administrators, not students. Students usually see only the similarity (plagiarism) report — if their institution shows them anything at all. If you've seen your own AI score, it was probably shared by a teacher or produced by a third-party service that runs Turnitin-style checks.

## Why human writing sometimes gets flagged

AI detectors look for statistical patterns, not for "proof." Text can look AI-like when it is:

- **Very formulaic** — standard essay templates, stock transitions ("Furthermore," "In conclusion"), definitions copied in your own words.
- **Heavily edited by tools** — grammar fixers and rewriting tools can smooth writing into the even, predictable style detectors associate with AI.
- **Written by a non-native speaker** — simpler vocabulary and careful, regular sentence structure can raise scores. Research on several detectors has found exactly this bias.
- **Short** — the less text there is, the more a few unusual sentences swing the result.

## What to do if your teacher asks about a \*% score

A \*% result is a weak signal, and Turnitin itself says AI scores should not be the only basis for any decision. Still, if you're asked about it:

1. **Stay calm and be factual.** An asterisk means a low amount of AI-like text was detected — that's all.
2. **Show your process.** Version history in Google Docs or Word, outlines, notes, earlier drafts and your sources are the strongest evidence of your own work.
3. **Explain your tools honestly.** If you used a grammar checker, translator or AI for brainstorming, say so — and check whether your course allowed it.
4. **Offer to talk through the work.** Being able to explain your argument and sources in your own words is persuasive.

## Can you lower an AI score?

The honest answer: write in your own voice and keep evidence of your process. Varying sentence length, adding concrete examples from your reading or lectures, and cutting filler phrases all make writing clearer *and* less template-like.

If you've drafted with AI help where your course allows it, a humanizer like the [DocMaxxing AI Humanizer](/humanizer) can rewrite stiff passages into more natural prose. No tool can promise a particular detector result, though — and you should be wary of any that does. Always read the output, fix anything that changed your meaning, and cite your sources.

## Key takeaways

- **\*%** means Turnitin found a small amount of AI-like text — too little to show a reliable number.
- The AI percentage covers qualifying prose only and is an estimate, not proof.
- Human writing can be flagged, especially formulaic, tool-edited or non-native writing.
- Your drafts, notes and version history are the best answer to any question about a score.
